🌱 Beginner guide — read this and you are a professional

  1. Need a paper? Search PubMed first. It lists 40M+ medical papers and links free copies. Example: type capsaicin neuropathy.
  2. Need to know if a treatment works? Read a Cochrane review. It combines every trial into one answer instead of one opinion.
  3. Need the real experiment? Open ClinicalTrials.gov and read the protocol + results. Companies must register here before starting — they cannot hide failures.
  4. Need official guidance? Check WHO for vaccines, outbreaks, and policy. Check MedlinePlus for plain-language explanations with zero ads.
  5. Need citations fast? Use Google Scholar to discover, then verify in PubMed/Cochrane before citing. Scholar finds everything but checks nothing.
  6. In China or citing Chinese work? Use CNKI + SinoMed first — Western indexes miss most Chinese journals.

Rule of thumb: discover on Scholar → verify on PubMed → decide with Cochrane → confirm the experiment on ClinicalTrials.gov → follow policy on WHO.

📄 Why clinicians don’t use arXiv (and where they go)

arXiv is physics/math/CS with a small biology corner and light screening. A wrong equation breaks code; a wrong dose harms patients. So medicine uses medRxiv (clinical/epidemiology/public health) and bioRxiv (genetics/neuroscience/cell). At the AI×medicine boundary, search both arXiv and medRxiv and say which you searched.

❓ FAQ (simple answers)

Is Scholar “bad”?

No — best for discovery, worst for validation (trust 59.0, last). Discover there, verify in PubMed/Cochrane.

Why is ClinicalTrials.gov #1?

Only it forces pre-registration — failed trials cannot disappear. Trust 100.0, transparency 10.

Why is Scopus 403?

Bot protection for guests. Humans with login pass; scripts get blocked. Trust stays high, machine-access low.

Can I cite this repo?

Cite the underlying platforms (PubMed, Cochrane…), plus this repo’s method + date. See README citation block.
